--- INSTAGRAM / FACEBOOK CAPTION (girls-who-play-baseball lead) ---

The off-season doesn't ask who belongs. It asks who kept going.

Mid-November. Fall ball is done, the fields are quiet, and nobody is checking rosters right now. For girls who play baseball, that quiet can actually be a gift. There's no dugout to prove anything to. There's just the work: a tee in the garage, a bucket of balls, a long-toss partner on Saturday morning.

In 35 years of coaching, the players who showed up different in the spring weren't the ones who played the most games. They were the ones who kept going when no one was keeping score, and wrote down what they did. More games don't make your player better. What they do between games, written down, does.

When spring comes and someone wonders whether your player belongs, the page already has the answer.

Tonight, one line: one thing your player worked on today, even if it was ten minutes.

Log it. Own it.
Coach
