The bat should fit today.

Every tryout I run, I can spot the too-big bat from the parking lot. The hands drop, the barrel drags, and a player with a good eye looks slow. Nobody taught them that swing. The bat did.

Holiday sales start next week, and a lot of bats get bought "to grow into." Before you buy, run two tests right in the store aisle:

1. Chest test (length): knob in the center of the chest, bat pointed out along the arm. If their hand reaches the end of the barrel, the length fits.
2. One-arm hold (weight): bat straight out to the side, one hand. If the barrel dips before 15 to 20 seconds, it's too heavy.

When two sizes both pass, take the smaller one. Bat speed beats bat size at every age. And check that the stamp (USA, USSSA, or BBCOR) matches your player's league.

The full size chart by age is on the MyGrind blog: "Youth Bat Sizing Guide." (Link in bio.)

Then let your player wear it out. One line tonight about the swings they took.

--- SOFTBALL PAIR CAPTION (for a second slot) ---

Bat speed beats bat size.

Holiday sales are next week, and the most common bat mistake in fastpitch is the same one in baseball: buying big so your player can grow into it. A heavy bat makes a long, late swing and weaker contact.

Buy the bat that fits now. Two quick checks: hand reaches the end of the barrel with the knob on the chest, and they can hold it straight out one-handed for 15 to 20 seconds. Then let your player wear it out, and size up when the tests say so.
