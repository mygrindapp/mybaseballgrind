The Test Most Families Never Hear About

I am a high school head coach again this year, which means I am back on the other side of the clipboard at tryouts. So let me tell you the thing I have never seen written down anywhere, in 35 years of doing this.

Most rosters are not decided by the best hitter or the biggest arm. They are decided by one repetition.

A coach tells a player to change something. Move the hands. Get the glove out front. Then we watch the very next rep.

The player who changes it goes in one column. The player who nods and does the same thing again goes in the other. That is the whole test, and almost nobody practices it.
 
What Actually Gets Measured

Three things, and they are probably not the three your player has been working on all summer.

Throwing accuracy, ahead of arm strength. Every coach notices a big arm. Every coach also knows a strong arm that sails throws costs them games.

First-step quickness, ahead of the 60 time. We time the 60 because it is easy to measure, not because it decides anything.

Clean hands, ahead of highlight plays. Anybody can lay out once and get a cheer. What decides innings is the routine ground ball fielded cleanly eleven times out of eleven.

Hitting gets the most attention from families and decides the least. Two rounds against a coach lobbing it in tells me almost nothing. I am watching the swing, not the results.
 
What A Tryout Means At Each Age

The word means something different depending on how old your player is. Here is what is actually being decided at each stop.

Youth, roughly 10U to 14U

Travel coaches are not picking for this season. They are picking for the next three, because they want to keep a group together. That is why the smaller kid with a great attitude gets taken over the bigger kid who is done growing. If your player is on the bubble, the thing that moves them is not another lesson. It is showing up like they want to be there.

High school

This is where cuts are real and the one-rep test above is running whether anyone says so or not. It is also where the first ten minutes matter more than they should. We are watching how they get off the bus, whether they know where to stand, whether they warm up or wait to be told. None of that is on the evaluation sheet. All of it is in the coach's head before the first ball is thrown.

College

There is no tryout anymore. The camp is the tryout. College staffs run their own camps specifically to evaluate, on their field, with their drills, and that is now the most direct way to be seen. If your player is a junior or senior who wants to keep playing, a day at a camp on a campus they actually like beats another highlight video by a mile. And at most levels, grades are doing as much work as the swing. That is a conversation to have in August, not in May of senior year.
 
Your Job That Week Is Smaller Than You Think

Handle the logistics so they only have to play. Cleats that fit, water, arrive early enough that they are not walking on at the last second. Take that whole load off them.

One short conversation the night before. Not a speech. Know where you are supposed to be, be first in line for one drill, and if a coach tells you to change something, change it on the very next rep.

One sentence in the car after. "I liked watching you play." Then let them talk if they want to, and let them be quiet if they do not.

And three things not to do.
Do not talk to the coach during tryouts. Nothing good happens there, and it lands on your player, not on you.
Do not narrate the other kids. They can hear you. So can the coach.
Do not ask "did you make it" in the parking lot. They do not know yet, and asking makes the not-knowing worse.
 
If They Do Not Make It

Some of them will not, and the ones who take it hardest are usually the ones who cared most.

The players I have watched come back from a cut share one thing. They got a real answer about why, and then they worked on that one specific thing for a year. Not on everything. On the one thing.

So ask the coach for the reason, calmly, a few days later, in private. Most of us will tell you honestly if you make it easy to.

The cut is information. Painful information, but information. What ruins players is not getting cut. What ruins them is getting cut and never being told why.
 
This Week's Journal Prompts

For your player: "What is one thing a coach has told me to fix that I have not fixed yet?" Then go fix it this week, and write down what changed.

For you: "What do I actually say in the car after a game, and is it helping?" Write the real sentence down, not the one you meant to say.

Here is the honest limit of the whole thing. I get two hours with a player at a tryout. In two hours I cannot see the tee work in the garage in July. I cannot see the long toss they did on their own three days a week when nobody made them. I am making a decision about someone's season on a very small window, and I know it.

The players who make that easier on me are the ones who can tell me exactly what they have been working on. Not a highlight video. A record. When a kid can be specific, it moves them from a name on a list to a player with a direction.
